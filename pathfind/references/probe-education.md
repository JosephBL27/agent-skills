# Probe: education

**Question:** what would you need to *learn* to do this well, and where does that
knowledge actually live?

This probe exists because of a specific blind spot: when a technique is newer
than training data, the confident answer and the correct answer diverge, and
nothing in the other probes catches it. Package search finds the library. It does
not tell you the community moved off that pattern eight months ago.

## When it is worth running

Run it when the domain is one you cannot name a current method for, when the
ecosystem moves fast (frontend, agent tooling, model APIs, anything with a
"the way to do this changed" reputation), or when you are about to build
something you have not built recently.

Skip it for stable domains. Nobody needs a video to write a SQL join.

## Sources, in the order that pays

**Official docs, changelogs, and release notes** answer "what does it do now"
faster than anything else, and they are authoritative. Start here.

**Video** answers a different question — "how do people actually do this" —
including the parts nobody writes down. Two skills own this:

- `/watch` — a specific video you already have a URL for. Downloads it, extracts
  frames, transcribes locally, and lets you ask questions about the content.
  Frames matter more than the transcript for anything demonstrated visually; a
  transcript of a screencast routinely omits the thing on screen.
- `learn-from-video` — the broader "how do people do X now" question, and turning
  what you find into a durable note or an installed capability.

**Conference talks and official course material** sit between the two: more
current than docs, more structured than a random tutorial.

Community discussion is supporting evidence. It is good for "is this a known
problem" and bad for "is this correct".

## Turn it into something durable

The point is not to watch a video. It is to end with knowledge that survives the
session. A finding worth keeping becomes a vault note or a skill — otherwise the
next session pays the same cost again.

## Reporting

Name what you would need to learn and where it lives. If the honest answer is
"nothing — this is well-trodden and stable", say that; it is a fast and useful
result. If the answer is "the technique postdates what I can rely on", say that
too, and treat it as blocking rather than proceeding on a confident guess.
